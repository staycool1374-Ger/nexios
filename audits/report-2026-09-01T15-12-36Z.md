## FINDINGS
- [S2] src/kernel/test/test_cap_mmio.cpp — assertion masking / Heisenbug race in `ioport_revoke_clears_live_bits` and `ioport_revoke_other_cap_keeps_bits`: the worker writes the harness signal globals (`g_handle`/`g_waiter`, `g_h_a`/`g_h_b`/`g_worker`) BOTH before the grant syscalls AND after them, while the comment claims "Signal the harness ONLY after the grant is installed".
  WHY: a timer tick preempting the worker in the few-instruction window between the early write and the `SYS_IOPORT_GRANT` call lets the harness exit its `while (g_waiter==nullptr || g_handle==0)` spin and assert `iopb_port_allowed` on a bitmap whose grant is not yet installed — a timing-dependent test failure that directly contradicts critical check #3.
- [S2] src/kernel/test/test_cap_mmio.cpp — use-after-free read in `ioport_ledger_dropped_at_cleanup`: after the `TaskPtr` scope destroys the TCB, the test dereferences the freed `g_tracked` pointer via `arch::iopb_port_allowed(*g_tracked, 0x60)`.
  WHY: `g_tracked` points at a cleaned-up/recycled TCB block; reading its `iopb_slot_` is undefined behavior and could flake or mislead if the block is reused between free and the assertion.
- [S3] src/kernel/syscall/syscall_handlers_mmio.cpp:83-92 — ledger-add-before-grant ordering does not roll back the reserved ledger entry if `iopb_grant_range` fails after `iopb_ledger_add` succeeds (phantom grant record until the cap is disposed).
  WHY: currently unreachable (a successful `iopb_claim` immediately precedes, with no intervening slot release), but the failure path leaves a stale record; reserve-then-rollback or grant-then-reserve would be strictly safer.
- [S3] src/kernel/memory/vmm.cpp (4 sites) — ENSURE relaxation `if (user && phys_addr < PMM::total_memory())` now permits mapping ANY `phys >= total_memory()` user-accessible without an `is_user_page` check.
  WHY: contained — only reachable via capability-gated `VMM::map_mmio_from_cap`, which rejects revoked/IO/zero-range caps — but the guard no longer distinguishes a real device BAR from arbitrary high physical memory; a hardening note.
